Waterborne bacterial infection

Cholera

Cholera is caused by Vibrio cholerae in unsafe water or food. It causes severe watery diarrhoea and fast dehydration. It is treatable with oral rehydration salts, and it is an active outbreak in Yangon and Rakhine now.

01. Background

A disease of unsafe water

Cholera spreads wherever drinking water is mixed with sewage. Without treatment a severe case can kill in hours from dehydration.

With oral rehydration salts (ORS) and, for severe cases, drip fluids, almost every patient survives.

Treatment facts

ORS saves lives

Mix ORS with clean water and sip often. Start at the first loose stool.

02. Transmission

How cholera spreads

Main route

1. Dirty drinking water

Water contaminated with the faeces of an infected person.

Food

2. Raw or uncooked food

Raw seafood, street food and food washed in unsafe water.

Floods

3. Rainy season and floods

Floods push sewage into wells, tanks and rivers.

03. Symptoms

Symptoms and warning signs

Watery diarrhoea

Pale, watery stool often called rice-water.

Vomiting

Often at the start of illness.

Leg cramps

Painful cramps from salt loss.

Thirst and weakness

Very dry mouth, sunken eyes, little urine.

Severe dehydration signs

A person can lose a litre of fluid an hour. Go to hospital at once for:

  • Cannot keep drinks down
  • Sunken eyes and very dry mouth
  • No urine for 6 hours or more
  • Extreme sleepiness or fainting

In Yangon call 192 for an ambulance. Read the emergency protocol.

04. Myanmar Context

Cholera in Myanmar

Between 9 and 16 August 2026 about 500 people were hospitalised with diarrhoea in Yangon and 149 tested positive for cholera. One death was reported. The Health Ministry also reported 82 diarrhoea cases across Rakhine and Mon as of 15 August, including 22 cholera cases and two deaths in Sittwe.

This follows a 2024 wave, when 576 hospitalised cases were reported in Yangon (Hlaingthayar) and 2,470 diarrhoea cases in Rakhine. In April 2026 the Health Cluster expected diarrhoea cases to rise sharply when the rains began.

Response seen in August 2026: Teams test water supplies, check chlorine levels, visit homes door to door, and hospitals prepare extra beds.

Household prevention

  • Drink only boiled, bottled or chlorinated water.
  • Wash hands with soap before eating and after the toilet.
  • Keep ORS at home and use it at the first loose stool.
