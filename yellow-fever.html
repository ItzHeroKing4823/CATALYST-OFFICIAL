Mosquito-borne virus

Yellow Fever

Yellow fever is a mosquito-borne virus that can damage the liver and turn the skin yellow. One vaccine dose protects for life. It does not normally occur in Myanmar, but travellers must know the rules.

01. Background

An old disease with a safe vaccine

Yellow fever caused deadly outbreaks in port cities for centuries. The vaccine, developed in the 1930s, is safe and effective.

Most patients have mild illness. About 1 in 7 develop severe illness, and about half of those die without care.

Vaccine facts

One dose for life

WHO says a single dose gives lifelong protection. The certificate is valid for life.

02. Transmission

How it spreads

Main route

1. Mosquito bites

Aedes and Haemogogus mosquitoes pass the virus from monkeys or people to people.

Where

2. Not in Myanmar

Local transmission has not been reported in Myanmar. The risk is for people who travel to a country with yellow fever.

Not person to person

3. Not by contact

Yellow fever does not spread from one person to another directly.

03. Symptoms

Symptoms and warning signs

Fever and chills

Sudden fever and headache.

Back and muscle pain

Strong aches and tiredness.

Nausea

Nausea and vomiting.

Yellow skin

Jaundice appears in severe cases.

Severe stage

A short recovery can be followed by a severe stage. Go to hospital at once for:

  • Yellow skin or eyes
  • Bleeding from the mouth, nose or eyes
  • Vomiting blood
  • Dark urine or little urine

In Yangon call 192 for an ambulance. Read the emergency protocol.

04. Myanmar Context

Yellow fever and Myanmar

Myanmar is not a yellow fever endemic country and no local cases are listed in the sources we use. The risk is for Myanmar residents who travel to a country with yellow fever risk, and for visitors who bring the virus in.

Myanmar has Aedes aegypti, the same mosquito that spreads dengue. Dengue control, such as emptying water containers weekly, also reduces the chance of any imported yellow fever spreading.

Typical public health response: Vaccination certificates checked at entry for travellers from risk countries, and mosquito control around airports.

Household prevention

  • Get vaccinated at least 10 days before travel to risk countries.
  • Keep your yellow certificate with your passport.
  • Prevent mosquito breeding at home as for dengue.
