Airborne bacterial infection

Tuberculosis (TB)

TB is caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis and mostly attacks the lungs. It spreads through the air when a patient coughs, and it can be cured with six months of medicine. TB is a serious health problem in Myanmar.

01. Background

An ancient disease we can cure

TB has been found in human remains thousands of years old.

Modern treatment cures most patients when every dose is taken for the full course. Stopping early causes drug-resistant TB, which is harder and longer to treat.

Treatment facts

Finish every dose

Drug-sensitive TB needs about 6 months of tablets. Stopping early lets resistant bacteria grow.

02. Transmission

How TB spreads

Main route

1. Coughing and sneezing

A person with lung TB releases tiny droplets that others breathe in.

Risk

2. Crowded, closed rooms

Poor airflow in homes, workplaces, prisons and camps raises the chance of spread.

Higher risk

3. Weak immunity

People with HIV, diabetes, malnutrition or who smoke are more likely to become ill.

03. Symptoms

Symptoms and warning signs

Long cough

A cough that lasts 2 weeks or more, sometimes with blood.

Night sweats

Soaking sweats while sleeping.

Weight loss

Losing weight without trying and poor appetite.

Fever and tiredness

Low fever and constant tiredness.

When to go to hospital

TB can become an emergency. Go to hospital at once for:

  • Coughing up blood
  • Severe shortness of breath
  • Chest pain with breathing
  • A stiff neck, headache and confusion

In Yangon call 192 for an ambulance. Read the emergency protocol.

04. Myanmar Context

TB in Myanmar

WHO estimates about 257,000 new TB cases and about 49,900 deaths a year in Myanmar (2022 data). About 13,000 cases a year are drug-resistant (MDR/RR-TB). Only 46% of estimated patients were treated.

Among new TB patients with a known HIV result, 6% were HIV-positive, and 72% of them were on HIV treatment. Everyone with TB should be offered an HIV test.

Typical public health response: Free TB testing and treatment through the National TB Programme, with a health worker watching or supporting each patient's treatment.

Household prevention

  • See a doctor for any cough lasting 2 weeks.
  • Open windows and keep rooms ventilated.
  • Take every dose until the doctor says to stop.
