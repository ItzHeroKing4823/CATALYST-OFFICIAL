Frequently Asked Questions

Heavy rain and floods mix sewage with drinking water. In August 2026 about 500 people were hospitalised with diarrhoea in Yangon in one week, and 149 tested positive for cholera. Boil or chlorinate drinking water and use oral rehydration salts at the first sign of watery diarrhoea.

Dengue cases rise during the rainy season, from about June to October. The Aedes mosquito bites in the daytime and breeds in water storage drums, flower vases and old tyres, so empty and scrub them every week.

Malaria is mostly a risk in forested and border areas of Myanmar, where cases have risen, not in the city itself. Test any fever before travelling back from these areas, and sleep under an insecticide-treated net when you visit them.

Routine measles vaccine coverage in Myanmar was 71% for the first dose and 68% for the second dose in 2025. Ask your nearest clinic whether your child has had both doses, and join any measles-rubella campaign that is offered.

Go to hospital at once for trouble breathing, chest pain, a stiff neck with fever, bleeding, repeated vomiting or severe dehydration. Call 192 for an ambulance in Yangon.
