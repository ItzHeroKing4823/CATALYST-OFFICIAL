Myanmar outbreaks

In August 2026 the Health Ministry reported about 500 people hospitalised with diarrhoea in Yangon in one week, 149 of them with confirmed cholera, plus cholera cases and deaths in Sittwe, Rakhine. Boil or chlorinate drinking water, wash hands with soap and use oral rehydration salts for watery diarrhoea.

Source: Bloomberg via The Star | Cholera profile

Malaria is concentrated in forested and border areas. In early 2026 cases passed the alert threshold in Tanintharyi and Southern Chin, and Kayin State has seen large rises since 2020. The UN Health Cluster also reports shortages of bed nets and test supplies.

Source: UN Myanmar Health Cluster | Malaria profile

Coverage was 71% for the first measles dose and 68% for the second dose in 2025, below the 95% needed to stop outbreaks. Rakhine and Chin are listed as high risk because no immunisation has taken place there in the past 5 years.

Source: WHO Myanmar EPI Factsheet 2026 | Measles profile

WHO estimates about 257,000 new TB cases a year (2022 data), and only 46% of patients were treated. TB is curable, and treatment is free through the National TB Programme. See a doctor for any cough lasting 2 weeks.

Source: WHO TB profile for Myanmar | TB profile

Infectious diseases and staying safe

An epidemic usually starts when a new pathogen passes from animals to humans and people have no immunity against it. It spreads faster when cities are crowded, cases are detected late, or people travel a lot.

Source: WHO | All references

Through droplets from coughing and sneezing, direct contact, tiny particles in rooms with poor airflow, dirty surfaces, mosquito bites, and unsafe water or food.

Source: WHO | All references

Stay home as soon as fever, loss of taste or smell, or breathing symptoms appear. Go to hospital immediately if there is shortness of breath, chest pressure, very high fever, or severe dehydration.

Sources: WHO, MoHS Myanmar | All references

Keep vaccinations up to date, open windows or use a fan for airflow, clean high-touch surfaces, wash hands with soap for 20 seconds, and wear a mask in crowded places during an outbreak.

Source: WHO | All references

The World Health Organization website, the Myanmar Ministry of Health and Sports announcements, and the references page of this website.

Sources: WHO, MoHS Myanmar | All references
