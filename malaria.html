Mosquito-borne parasite

Malaria

Malaria is caused by Plasmodium parasites spread by the female Anopheles mosquito at night. It causes cycles of fever, chills and sweating, and it is rising again in parts of Myanmar.

AnophelesMosquito that spreads malariaBites from dusk to dawn
7-30 daysIncubation periodLonger for P. vivax

01. Background

An old disease that returned

Malaria has infected people for thousands of years. Quinine, and later artemisinin, saved millions of lives.

The Greater Mekong region, which includes Myanmar, is where parasites first became resistant to artemisinin. Treatment must be finished fully and taken only after a positive test.

Vector facts

A night biter

The Anopheles mosquito bites between dusk and dawn, so a treated bed net is the best protection.

02. Transmission

How malaria spreads

Main route

1. Anopheles bites

A mosquito picks up the parasite from an infected person and passes it to the next person it bites.

Where

2. Forest and border areas

Transmission is highest near forests, plantations, rivers and mining camps, where people sleep outdoors.

Rare route

3. Blood and mother to baby

Malaria can pass through infected blood or from mother to baby, but this is rare.

03. Symptoms

Symptoms and warning signs

Fever and chills

Shaking chills followed by high fever, often in cycles.

Sweating

Heavy sweating as the fever drops.

Headache and aches

Headache, body pain and tiredness.

Vomiting

Nausea and vomiting, sometimes with diarrhoea.

Severe malaria signs

Falciparum malaria can become life-threatening within a day or two. Go to hospital at once for:

  • Confusion, fits or deep sleepiness
  • Very yellow skin or eyes
  • Fast or difficult breathing
  • Dark urine or very little urine

In Yangon call 192 for an ambulance. Read the emergency protocol.

04. Myanmar Context

Malaria in Myanmar

In December 2025 the UN Health Cluster reported further expansion of malaria cases throughout Myanmar, with severe shortages of bed nets and of testing and treatment supplies. In the first quarter of 2026, cases rose above the alert threshold in Tanintharyi and Southern Chin.

A 2023 report described malaria cases in Kayin State rising about ten-fold since 2020. Risk is highest in forested border areas. Yangon city itself has little malaria, so any fever after travel from these areas needs a test.

Typical public health response: Free rapid tests and treatment at township health centres, village health workers, and treated bed nets.

Household prevention

  • Sleep under an insecticide-treated bed net.
  • Wear long sleeves and use repellent at dusk and dawn.
  • Test every fever after travel to forest or border areas.
