Emergency Protocol

What to do in a medical emergency

Know the numbers, the warning signs and the first steps before you need them. In a life-threatening emergency, call for help first and read later.

Myanmar emergency numbers

Call these numbers first

192

Ambulance

Medical emergencies and transport to hospital.

191

Fire Service

Fires, rescue and natural disasters.

199

Police

Accidents, violence and danger to life.

Please note: ambulance coverage and waiting times can differ between areas. If you cannot get through, ask someone nearby to call, or go to the nearest emergency department. You can also call a hospital’s own emergency line from the Hospital Directory.

Do not wait

Call for help straight away if you see

  • Trouble breathing, or breathing that is very fast or noisy
  • Chest pain or pressure that does not go away
  • Fainting, or a person who does not wake up or respond
  • A seizure (fit), especially the first one or one that lasts a long time
  • Heavy bleeding that does not stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure
  • Sudden confusion, weakness on one side, a drooping face or trouble speaking
  • Very high fever with a stiff neck, severe headache, rash or confusion
  • Severe dehydration: very dry mouth, no urine, sunken eyes or extreme sleepiness
Step by step

The emergency protocol

Stay calm and stay safe

Move away from danger such as traffic, fire or electric wires. Do not put yourself at risk. If the person might have a spreading infection, put on a mask before you get close.

Call for help

Call 192 for an ambulance. Say where you are (township, street and a nearby landmark), what happened, the person’s age, and whether they are awake and breathing. Give your phone number and stay on the line until the operator says you can hang up.

While you wait

Keep the person lying down and comfortable. Do not give food or drink to someone who is drowsy or unconscious. Loosen tight clothing, unlock the door and turn on the lights. Gather their medicines, allergies list and ID. If they are not breathing normally and you are trained in CPR, start it. The operator can guide you.

If an infection is possible

Ask the sick person to wear a mask and sit apart from others in a room with open windows. Wash your hands with soap often. Tell the operator and the hospital about fever, cough, diarrhoea or rash, and about any recent contact with a sick person or travel, so the staff can get ready before you arrive.

At the hospital

Bring your phone, ID and the medicines list. Follow the triage staff’s instructions. Keep wearing a mask and stay apart from other patients until a doctor says it is not needed. Afterwards, tell close contacts if the doctor says they should watch for symptoms.

Outbreak illnesses

Quick guides for common illnesses

General guidance based on WHO advice. It is not a diagnosis.

Cholera and severe watery diarrhoea

Start oral rehydration solution (ORS) straight away and give small sips often. Go to a hospital quickly if the diarrhoea is very watery, there is repeated vomiting, or the person is very sleepy or has sunken eyes. Wash hands with soap and drink only boiled or treated water.

Dengue

Rest and drink plenty of fluids. Do not take aspirin or ibuprofen unless a doctor says so. Go to hospital at once if, after the fever starts to fall, there is severe belly pain, repeated vomiting, bleeding from the gums or nose, black stools or restlessness.

Malaria

Fever with chills or sweats after time in a malaria area needs a blood test the same day. Confusion, a seizure, dark urine, yellow eyes or extreme weakness are emergency signs. Go to hospital immediately.

Bird flu and other severe chest infections

Fever and cough after touching sick or dead poultry is a reason to call the clinic first and say so. Wear a mask, do not touch birds, and keep away from other people. Go to hospital at once if breathing becomes difficult.

Prepare today

Be ready before an emergency

  • Save 192 (ambulance), 191 (fire) and 199 (police) in your phone.
  • Know your nearest hospital: find it in the Hospital Directory.
  • Keep a card with your medicines, allergies and blood group in your wallet or phone.
  • Keep ORS sachets, a thermometer, masks and a basic first-aid kit at home.
  • Agree on a plan with your family: who calls, who stays with the patient, how you travel.
