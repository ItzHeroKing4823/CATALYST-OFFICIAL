Bird virus

Avian Flu (H5N1)

H5N1 is a bird flu virus that can infect people who handle sick poultry. Human cases are rare but about half of known cases have died, so early hospital care matters.

Many households keep chickens and ducks, so contact with sick birds is the main way people get infected.

01. Background

From birds to people

H5N1 mainly infects wild and farm birds. In Myanmar it has been reported on poultry farms and once in a person, in 2007.

The virus does not spread easily between people. The worry is that it could change and begin to do so.

Poultry facts

Report sick birds

Sudden deaths of many chickens or ducks should be reported to the local livestock office.

02. Transmission

How it spreads

Main route

1. Sick or dead birds

Touching infected birds, their droppings or feathers.

Risk

2. Live bird markets

Slaughtering and plucking birds at home or in markets.

Rare route

3. Very close care

Spread between people has happened only after long, close care of a patient.

03. Symptoms

Symptoms and warning signs

Fever

High fever over 38 C.

Cough and sore throat

Cough, sore throat and runny nose.

Aches

Muscle pain and headache.

Hard breathing

Quick progress to pneumonia.

When to go to hospital

Anyone with fever and cough after touching sick poultry should go to hospital and say so. Go at once for:

  • Trouble breathing
  • Chest pain
  • Fever lasting more than 3 days
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea with respiratory signs

In Yangon call 192 for an ambulance. Read the emergency protocol.

04. Myanmar Context

Bird flu in Myanmar

Myanmar confirmed its first human H5N1 case in December 2007. It was a 7-year-old girl from Kyaing Tong Township in Shan State who fell ill in November 2007 and recovered. Myanmar has also reported H5N1 outbreaks on poultry farms to the World Organisation for Animal Health.

No recent human cases are listed in the sources we use. Risk comes from backyard poultry, live bird markets and trade across the borders.

Typical public health response: Culling of infected flocks, movement limits, disinfection, and antiviral treatment for exposed people.

Household prevention

  • Do not touch sick or dead birds.
  • Cook chicken and eggs fully.
  • Wash hands with soap after handling poultry.
