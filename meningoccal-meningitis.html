Meningococcal Meningitis

Meningococcal meningitis is a bacterial infection of the lining around the brain and spinal cord. It can kill within 24 hours, so a stiff neck with fever is always an emergency.

01. Background

A disease that moves fast

Meningococcal bacteria live in the nose and throat of about 1 in 10 healthy people. Rarely they enter the blood and brain.

Without quick antibiotics the disease can kill within a day. Survivors may have hearing loss, brain damage or limb loss.

Care facts

Hours matter

Antibiotics should start as soon as the disease is suspected, even before test results.

02. Transmission

How it spreads

Main route

1. Close contact

Coughing, kissing and sharing cups or cutlery pass the bacteria through saliva droplets.

Risk

2. Crowded living

Dormitories, camps and mass gatherings raise the risk.

Prevention

3. Vaccines

Several vaccines protect against the main types of the bacterium.

03. Symptoms

Symptoms and warning signs

Stiff neck

Pain and stiffness when bending the neck forward.

High fever

Sudden high fever and chills.

Bad headache

Severe headache and dislike of bright light.

Purple rash

A rash of purple spots that does not fade when pressed with a glass.

This is always an emergency

Do not wait at home. Go to hospital at once for:

  • Stiff neck with fever
  • A rash that does not fade under a glass
  • Confusion or deep sleepiness
  • A baby who is very sleepy, floppy or has a bulging soft spot

In Yangon call 192 for an ambulance. Read the emergency protocol.

04. Myanmar Context

Meningitis and Myanmar

No national outbreak figures for Myanmar are published in the sources we use. The main local risks are crowded living places and travel.

Myanmar pilgrims who travel abroad for a pilgrimage may need proof of ACWY meningococcal vaccination. Yangon's Waibargi Infectious Disease Hospital and the general hospitals in our directory treat suspected cases.

Typical public health response: Immediate antibiotics for the patient and preventive antibiotics for close contacts such as family members.

Household prevention

  • Ask your doctor about the ACWY vaccine before a pilgrimage abroad.
  • Do not share cups, bottles or cutlery.
  • Tell a doctor at once if a close contact had meningitis.
