Bloodborne virus

HIV and AIDS

HIV is a virus that weakens the immune system. With daily treatment people live long, healthy lives and cannot pass the virus to partners once it is undetectable. Without treatment HIV can lead to AIDS.

01. Background

From a death sentence to a manageable condition

HIV was identified in the early 1980s. Antiretroviral therapy (ART) became widely available from the late 1990s.

People on effective treatment have a near-normal life expectancy. Stopping treatment lets the virus rebound within weeks.

Treatment facts

Test and treat

Early testing and daily medicine keep the virus too low to damage the body or pass on.

03. Symptoms

Symptoms and warning signs

Flu-like illness

Fever, sore throat and rash 2 to 4 weeks after infection.

Swollen glands

Lumps in the neck, armpits or groin.

Weight loss

Long-lasting diarrhoea and weight loss.

Frequent infections

Repeated thrush, TB or pneumonia.

Get medical help urgently

A person with HIV who has these signs needs care quickly. Go to hospital for:

  • A cough for more than 2 weeks (possible TB)
  • Severe headache and confusion
  • Severe diarrhoea with weight loss
  • Trouble breathing

In Yangon call 192 for an ambulance. Read the emergency protocol.

04. Myanmar Context

HIV in Myanmar

In 2024 antiretroviral treatment was maintained for 220,000 clients affected by conflict. 60% of them had a viral load test and 95% of those tested were virally suppressed. PrEP expanded to 10 more sites and reached nearly 5,000 people at higher risk.

Care for over 18,000 people who inject drugs improved through 35 methadone clinics. TB is the biggest killer of people with HIV, so test for both.

Typical public health response: Free testing and ART at public clinics and community groups, methadone clinics, and PrEP for people at higher risk.

Household prevention

  • Get tested and know your status.
  • Use condoms and never share needles.
  • Take ART every day and attend all clinic visits.
