Airborne viral infection

Measles

Measles is one of the most contagious viruses. It causes fever and a red rash and can lead to pneumonia and brain swelling. Two doses of vaccine give strong protection.

High risk areas Rakhine and Chin

The UN Health Cluster listed Rakhine and Chin as high risk. No immunisation has been done there in the past 5 years.

01. Background

A preventable disease

Before vaccines, measles caused millions of deaths every year. Vaccination has prevented most of them.

The virus stays in the air for up to two hours after a patient leaves a room. Because it is so contagious, about 95% of people must be vaccinated to stop outbreaks.

Vaccine facts

Two doses

The first dose is given at 9 months and the second dose later in the routine schedule.

02. Transmission

How measles spreads

Main route

1. Breathing infected air

Coughs and sneezes release virus that can stay in a room for hours.

Risk

2. Unvaccinated children

Children who missed vaccine doses catch measles first.

Risk

3. Crowded places

Camps, schools and busy clinics let measles spread fast.

03. Symptoms

Symptoms and warning signs

High fever

Fever up to 40 C that starts first.

Cough and runny nose

Cough, runny nose and red, watery eyes.

Red rash

Rash starts on the face and spreads down the body.

White spots

Tiny white spots inside the mouth before the rash.

Complications

Measles can cause pneumonia, diarrhoea and brain swelling, especially in small children. Go to hospital at once for:

  • Fast or hard breathing
  • Fits or extreme sleepiness
  • Signs of dehydration
  • A child with measles who is also very thin or ill

In Yangon call 192 for an ambulance. Read the emergency protocol.

04. Myanmar Context

Measles in Myanmar

Myanmar reported 5,252 measles cases in 2019. Coverage then collapsed during 2021, when only 44% of children got dose 1. By 2025 coverage was 71% for dose 1 and 68% for dose 2, still below the 95% needed. Reported cases were 6 in 2025, but low reports can also mean weak testing.

A 2025 catch-up campaign for children aged 9 to 59 months reached 67%. In April 2026 the Health Cluster warned that Rakhine and Chin are at high risk of measles.

Typical public health response: Measles-rubella catch-up campaigns, routine vaccination at health centres, and vitamin A for sick children.

Household prevention

  • Check your child's vaccine card for 2 doses.
  • Join measles-rubella campaigns when offered.
  • Keep a child with fever and rash at home and call a clinic.
