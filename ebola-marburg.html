Bloodborne viruses

Ebola and Marburg

Ebola and Marburg are rare, severe filovirus diseases spread through body fluids. Outbreaks need fast isolation, protective clothing and contact tracing. No case has been reported in Myanmar.

01. Background

Rare but deadly

Ebola and Marburg are rare but very severe viral haemorrhagic fevers. They spread through the blood and body fluids of a sick person.

Fruit bats are the likely natural hosts. No case has been reported in Myanmar.

Response facts

Fast isolation

Isolating patients, tracing contacts and safe burials are how outbreaks end.

02. Transmission

How they spread

Main route

1. Body fluids

Blood, vomit, urine or sweat of a sick patient, through broken skin or eyes, nose and mouth.

Risk

2. Caring for patients

Family carers and health workers without protective gear.

Risk

3. Funerals

Touching the body of someone who died of the disease.

03. Symptoms

Symptoms and warning signs

Sudden fever

High fever and extreme tiredness.

Aches

Muscle pain, headache and sore throat.

Vomiting and diarrhoea

Often severe.

Bleeding

Bleeding inside and outside the body in late stages.

If you have been in an outbreak area

Anyone with fever within 21 days of returning from an outbreak area must call a hospital before going in. Tell staff about the travel. Warning signs:

  • Fever after travel to an outbreak area
  • Vomiting blood or bleeding
  • Contact with a patient or body
  • Severe weakness

In Yangon call 192 for an ambulance. Read the emergency protocol.

04. Myanmar Context

Ebola, Marburg and Myanmar

No Ebola or Marburg case has been reported in Myanmar. The country's risk is imported cases from travellers returning from outbreak areas.

Yangon's Waibargi Infectious Disease Hospital, listed in our directory, is the city's specialist infectious disease hospital. During outbreaks elsewhere, health authorities can strengthen airport health checks.

Typical public health response: Screening of travellers, isolation wards, and tracing everyone a patient met for 21 days.

Household prevention

  • Avoid contact with the blood or body fluids of sick people.
  • Do not touch bats or bushmeat in outbreak areas.
  • Report fever after travel immediately.
